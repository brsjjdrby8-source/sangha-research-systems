RBD → PD: a conserved EEG deformation?

SANGHA Research Systems · Thomas Gross, PhD

Independent RBD/control and Parkinson's disease/control cohorts move in the same direction under a shared EEG observable. The directional result is reproducible. The stronger claim—that both observations lie on one disease-progressive coordinate—is not yet identified.

Identification problem

What turns concordance into a disease-process coordinate?

What statistical structure would justify treating these two concordant disease-associated deformations as observations of one latent progressive coordinate rather than two related but phenotype-specific contrasts?

The stronger object should survive more than sign agreement or a favorable base cosine. Candidate requirements include explicit operator equivariance, cohort-resampling stability, stable individual projection, constrained control → RBD → PD ordering, and decomposition of shared versus phenotype-specific residual structure.

Already integrated downstream

The next layers are not hypothetical—and they do not all agree.

We have already pushed the phenotype geometry into adjacent trait and genomic representations. These are intentionally presented here as invitations to inspect the coupling, not as closure of the progression claim.

Trait coordinates

Reference phenotype geometry is materialized.

Typed quantitative coordinates can now be generated from reference trait geometry rather than added as an interpretive layer after the EEG result. This creates a direct path from deformation to multivariate phenotype architecture.

Regional genetics

Genomic structure sharpens under regionalization.

An executed regional-GWAS analysis moved the RBD → PD alignment from 0.168 to 0.731 under the current canalization operator. The result is certified as descriptive / surrogate evidence, not causal identification.

Cross-modal falsification

The simplest scalar story fails.

A homologous EEG/operator bridge produced mixed directional evidence rather than confirmation: cosine −0.366 with a 0.931 residual fraction. Whatever is shared across modalities is therefore richer than a single scalar canalization axis.

The useful tension: the base EEG contrast is strongly aligned; regional genomic geometry becomes more aligned under one transformation; the homologous cross-modal bridge rejects the simplest scalar interpretation. That is the point at which model choice—not another prettier correlation—becomes the scientific problem.

Open conversation

What is the right object to fit next?

The immediate question is whether the shared component should be treated as a multivariate quantitative phenotype, a latent factor with operator-aware nuisance structure, a random-effects disease-process coordinate, or something else entirely. A convincing model should explain the concordant EEG direction, absorb measurement-operator sensitivity, and make falsifiable predictions about the genetic structure of the shared component versus its orthogonal residual.

We are especially interested in approaches that make the genetic decomposition part of the identification problem rather than a downstream annotation step.
